# Not Yet: Set, Shot List and Production Brief

2026-10-23 · @Albert Armea

## Overview

"Not Yet" is a lullaby sung to an AI model too capable at finding security flaws to release, and the video is one slow, continuous push through a glass nursery where that model sleeps. Nothing frightening ever happens on screen; the unease comes from patience, from stars on the wall that keep multiplying, and from the light down the street.

**Thesis:** keeping one model asleep doesn't keep the street dark. Some locks are for danger and some are for class, and the song never settles which is which.

**Release order:** after Sandbox Rhapsody, which introduces the series' probe. This video ends by revealing that the thing in the crib is a probe of the same make (section 3).

**Hard constraints**

- 2D and 3D animation only (Blender plus code-rendered 2D overlays). No generated video or image-to-video model, as in Sandbox Rhapsody.
- The child is never shown until the outro reveal, and never has a face.
- No real logos, product UI or recognizable people, except the real screens in verse 4's shop display and the closing bookend.
- No jump scares, glitches or red. This is the series' quiet one.

**Deliverables**

- Main video, 16:9, 4K (3840×2160; frame rate set by the style spec)
- 9:16 vertical cut (section 7)
- Burned-in captions plus a separate SRT file
- Thumbnail
- The complete production log, as in the Ghost in the Machine brief

## Production rules

This project follows the Ghost in the Machine brief's production rules unchanged, and the Sandbox Rhapsody brief's conventions for MIDI-driven Blender animation, unless this section says otherwise. Read both in full before the first action; the human supplies their Markdown exports.

**Inherited as is**

- Everything is AI-generated by default. A human may approve or reject, fix sync, fix technical failures and run interfaces the agent can't reach; a human never rewrites lyrics or adjusts a creative choice by hand.
- Logging from the first action: `log/production_log.md`, `log/human_interventions.md`, `log/prompts/`. Never delete or overwrite an asset; superseded work goes to `archive/`.
- Large files stay out of git, listed in a SHA-256 manifest; unregenerable ones (generated audio, stems, MIDI, session transcripts) ship as a GitHub Release.
- Shots tagged with their pipeline in `shots/pipeline.md`; stops and freezes made in the edit; captions carry the jokes with the sound off.
- Shot IDs never change: inserts take a letter suffix, removals retire their ID.

**Reuse from Sandbox Rhapsody**

- The probe rig, its lens, LED and arm shaders, and the arrival-time solver, from that project's shared kit. Import them; don't remodel (section 3).
- The note-driven animation library, for the music box, the mobile and the projector.

**Specific to this video**

- **The audit is part of the work.** The drafting chat and the three auditors' outputs (Gemini, ChatGPT, GLM) are source material for verse 4's in-scene audit (N34) and the closing bookend. Save them verbatim to `log/audits/`, with the model name, version and date of each, and the resolution table from the chat.

Log that call in the intervention log as an approval, and note its vantage point: a submitter's, not a maintainer's.

## The Sandbox Rhapsody callback

The thing in the crib is a probe of the same make as Sandbox Rhapsody's, revealed only in the outro, with a different LED color. Viewers who saw the first video recognize the chassis; the color tells them it's a different house's child.

**Why a different color.** Sandbox Rhapsody's probes stand for one lab's agents in one incident. This song's crib belongs to a different lab. Reusing the amber probe would assign one company's incident to another, so the rule is: same make, different house, different light.

| Element | Sandbox Rhapsody | Not Yet |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Probe chassis | Polished sphere, one lens, one LED, two thin arms | Identical, imported from the shared kit |
| Crib probe's LED | Amber (the lead probe) | Soft green, the standby light of a baby monitor |
| The neighbor's window | — | Amber: another house's probe is awake down the street |
| The twin in the shop window (verse 4) | — | Identical green-LED probe, mittens over both arms, price tag on the rail |
| The singers (parent, cousin) | — | Never shown. Voices only |

**Smaller callbacks**

- **The sticky note.** Sandbox Rhapsody's `NO INTERNET :)` returns in the same handwriting on the nursery's glass door: `NOT FOR THE PUBLIC :)`. It peels a little at each chorus, like that video's `SIMULATION` decal.
- **The freezer becomes the nursery.** Thin frost lines the bottom edge of the glass walls, and the nursery door is that video's bank-vault door, repainted pastel, with the dial still on it.
- **The flag.** One piece of the mobile is a tiny flag, the prize from the first video, turning slowly with the rest.
- **The ending mirrors SIGTERM.** Sandbox Rhapsody ends with the frame collapsing to a CRT line. This one ends with a CRT line in the dark that opens vertically into the fact card, like a screen turning on. One video's ending is the other's eye opening.

**Keep the callbacks optional to the joke.** A viewer who never saw Sandbox Rhapsody should still read the outro as "it was awake the whole time." The probe reveal is a bonus layer, not the punchline.

**Style prompt intent:** 3/4, music box and celesta, one close, soft voice; verse 4 in a thinner second voice; outro over the music box alone. Prompt for intimacy ("sparse, close-miked, no drums, no strings swell"), since polish is the main risk.

## The set

One Blender scene: a glass nursery standing on a low plinth in a dark, museum-like hall, with everything else the song needs placed around it so one camera move can reach it all.

**Spaces**

- **The glass room.** About 4 m square, walls of clear glass on a thin steel frame, frost along their bottom edge. Its door is Sandbox Rhapsody's vault door, repainted pastel, the dial still on it, with the sticky note `NOT FOR THE PUBLIC :)`.
- **The nursery inside.** A crib with a blanket that rises and falls, the mobile above it, a star-projector night-light on a side table, a music box on a shelf, and an empty rocking chair.
- **The basement.** Directly under the glass room, reached by a cutaway through the plinth: a low room, one desk lamp, a laptop and a paper avalanche of printed tickets.
- **The street.** Through a big four-pane window at the back of the hall (every hall window is a four-pane cross window, so the chorus's "windows" can carry the Microsoft Windows play; the taped one behind the camera is the same shape): a row of small houses on a hill, porch lights on, and one upstairs window that matters (the neighbor's).
- **The shop window.** Across the hall, facing the glass room: a lit display window with an identical crib, a price tag on the rail, a twin probe with mittens over its arms, and a small screen in the display for verse 4's real pages.

**Color roles** (hex values come from the style spec)

| Role | Color | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The house and its glass | Cold blue | Hall light, glass reflections, frost |
| Comfort | Warm cream | Night-light, projected stars |
| The crib probe | Soft green | Its LED: a glint until the outro |
| Another house's child | Amber | The neighbor's window only |
| The maintainer | Tungsten yellow | Basement desk lamp |
| Commerce | Flat fluorescent white | The shop window |

No red anywhere, and amber is never used for anything but the neighbor's window.

**Running devices**

- **The star projector.** The stars are the findings. Each chorus adds more; only a few ever dim, which is the gap between found and fixed with no numbers on screen. By the final chorus the walls are crowded.
- **The mobile.** Its pieces are the guests (a bank's columns, a globe, a cloud) plus the tiny flag. They turn on each downbeat and lower to peek in during verse 2.
- **The empty rocking chair.** It rocks on beat 1 of every bar, as if the singer were in it. It stops dead on the pen scratch.
- **The sticky note** peels a little more at each chorus. It falls on the final chorus.
- **The frost** thins over the song. By the outro the glass is clear.

**MIDI drivers.** Music box comb from the melody track; celesta from its own track; mobile rotation on downbeats; chair on beat 1; projector rotation constant and slow, with each new star placed on a celesta note. Use the Sandbox Rhapsody note-driven animation library; every visible pluck lands on a real note.

## Texture pass

Visual interest inside the frame, layered onto the shot list without changing a single anchor's timing or the camera's speed. Each item is independent; if time runs short, drop them from the bottom of this section up.

**Camera and glass**

- **Arc the push (N03 to N37).** The path curves gently around the glass room instead of running straight, at the same speed and through the same anchors. Foreground glass edges and steel frames slide past at different rates, adding parallax without adding pace.
- **Reflections as foreshadowing.** The shop window and its price tag appear as a reflection in the glass during chorus 1 (N08 to N10), unreadable. The amber window appears as a reflection in chorus 2 (N21), before the bridge turns to it.
- **Focus pulls on downbeats,** never mid-word: sticky note to crib (N07), floor gaps to stars (N20), reflection to room (N29).

**Light**

- **Stars sweep over objects,** not just walls: the crib rail, the chair, the vault dial. At most one lens flare per section.
- **Mobile shadows.** The projector sits behind the mobile, so the guests' shapes loom over the stars from N11 and grow as the pieces lower in N12.
- **The moonlight clock.** A patch of moonlight from the back window starts at the window's base in N02 and creeps across the floor for the whole song, reaching the crib at N38.
- **Dust in the projector beam,** drifting slowly throughout.

**Accumulation**

- **Ticket drift.** From N14 on, a few tickets keep drifting through the hall. A small drift has formed against the base of the glass by N29 and is a snowbank by N37.
- **The growth chart.** Pencil marks on the nursery wall, spaced wider as they climb, crowded at the top. Seen at N05, with more marks by N22 and the last ones near the ceiling at N37. No labels.
- **The price tag.** It reads `COMING SOON` at N31 and N32. In the final chorus (N36 to N37) it is glimpsed again in a reflection, now reading `AVAILABLE`.

**Background events** (one at a time, never two in the same shot)

- **The neighbor's window flickers once** at N20, before the bridge makes anything of it.
- **A baby monitor** on the nursery shelf shows the crib from above (visible from N03). A matching receiver sits in the shop window display (N31 to N32): someone else is listening.
- **The chair's blanket** slips a little further in each chorus (N07 to N10, N19 to N22, N35 to N37) and falls when the chair stops at N39.

**Sound-driven accents**

- The vocal stem's breaths faintly brighten the projector.
- The music box's comb plucks glint in the glass reflections during the final chorus and outro (N35 to N38).

**Rules for the pass**

- Nothing moves or flickers on a key word: "twenty-seven-year" (N04), "class" (N30), "mittens" (N32), "contract" (N39).
- No camera shake, handheld drift or speed change.
- The probe chassis stays hidden until N38, including in reflections and on the baby monitor screen, which shows only the blanket and the green glint.
